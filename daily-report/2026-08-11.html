3. Signal worth writing about today

Security questionnaires just grew an AI-specific branch — 320 new questions, and jsdoc-scribe's "no AI" architecture is a shortcut through a big chunk of them

This is the strongest unused signal in today's research, and it directly extends ground the blog already owns rather than starting cold: the 2026-08-07 post ("Answering the Security Questionnaire, Line by Line") mapped the general CAIQ v4 domains to jsdoc-scribe's facts. Since then, CSA shipped AICM v1.1 (2026-06-23) — 247 controls, 18 domains, a new Model & Data Security domain — with a dedicated AI-CAIQ v1.1 self-assessment layer of 320 questions specifically for vendors whose products touch AI in any way. This is now a distinct, larger assessment surface that a growing share of procurement teams will start attaching to vendor reviews alongside the base CCM/CAIQ, per CSA's own FAQ and independent trackers (Vorp Labs, GetAIGovernance). The base CCM/CAIQ track also moved independently to v4.1 (283 questions, 207 controls), with CAIQ v4.0.x staying valid only until January 2028 — a dated fact worth stating plainly rather than left implicit.

The angle that hasn't been used yet: most vendors evaluating whether they need to fill out AI-CAIQ get tripped up because their product touches AI somewhere in the stack (an AI-assisted feature, an LLM-based helper, a vendor dependency that calls a model API) and now has to answer 320 detailed questions about data handling, model provenance, and training-data lineage. jsdoc-scribe's answer to the applicability question itself is short: it doesn't call an AI model at any point in its pipeline (TypeScript compiler API used purely as a parser), so most of AI-CAIQ's MDS-domain and AI-specific-risk questions are answered by "not applicable — no AI, no LLM calls, no training pipeline, no model" rather than requiring a paragraph of qualification per question. That's a sharper, more current version of the 2026-08-07 piece's argument, now grounded in a framework that's six weeks old rather than the general 2025-era CAIQ baseline.
