3. Signal worth writing about today

Bus factor has real, citable measurement methodology now — and a hard number for what losing the fight costs

Bus factor (aka truck factor) research has matured well past the informal definition: 2026 coverage cites a "silo ratio" metric (% of meaningful commits to a file/module made by the single top contributor — 95% silo ratio means one person owns nearly everything), Avelino's greedy Truck Factor algorithm for estimating impact of removing top contributors, and a dedicated tool (Bus Factor Explorer, arXiv:2403.08038) for computing and visualizing it per-repo. Consensus recommendation across sources: a bus factor of 3-5+ per critical module is the target; 1 is the danger zone.

Paired with it: 2026 onboarding-cost data now puts the true first-90-days cost of a new engineer at $20K-$80K (salary is only ~40% of that figure — the rest is mentor time, tooling, and team velocity drag), and new hires reach only 25% productivity in their first 30 days without structured onboarding, taking up to six months to reach full productivity on a large, under-documented codebase.

Sources: How to Measure Bus Factor in Your Software Team — Calyntro · Bus Factor: The Risk Metric Most Teams Ignore Until It Hurts — Leader Menu · Bus Factor Explorer — arXiv:2403.08038 · Bus Factor: A Human-Centered Risk Metric — Cesar Soto Valero · Developer Onboarding Cost 2026 · 150+ Onboarding Statistics: 2026 Verified Report — Gitnux
