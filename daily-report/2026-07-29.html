3. Signal worth writing about today

The EU AI Act's high-risk provisions go fully enforceable in four days — and the compliance stack around it is already stacking three more regulations on top

The EU AI Act's high-risk AI system obligations become fully enforceable August 2, 2026 — this Sunday. Fines reach up to €35M or 7% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher. It doesn't land alone: NIS2 and DORA are already in force and overlapping, meaning a single vendor relationship can require a GDPR data processing agreement, a DORA ICT service contract with audit rights and an exit plan, a NIS2 supplier security assessment, and AI Act due diligence if any AI component is involved — four separate evidence chains for one tool.

Sources: NIS2, DORA, and the EU AI Act — Kiteworks · DORA, NIS2 and the EU AI Act Making Sovereign Cloud Mandatory — SoftwareSeni · Demonstrable Compliance in 2026 — Msafe

Security questionnaires are now the single biggest deal-killer in B2B SaaS procurement, and the "does it touch AI" question is the newest line item

78% of companies report security reviews caused deal delays in the past year (Vanta State of Trust Report). SIG questionnaires in 2026 run from ~130 questions (SIG Lite) to ~800 (SIG Core). A recurring failure mode called out in 2026 procurement coverage: "IT sent back a security questionnaire the vendor can't fully answer, and now your deal is stalled at the finish line" — specifically because AI-agent and AI-assisted tooling adds a data-governance question set (where is data processed, does it leave the EEA, what third-party model receives it) that most vendors weren't built to answer cleanly.

Sources: Vendor Security Questionnaire Response Playbook 2026 — DevBrows · The 2026 AI Procurement Checklist for B2B SaaS — Docket · Security Questionnaire Template 2026: CAIQ, SIG & DDQ — SaaSFort
