3. Signal worth writing about today

ESLint quietly removed its own JSDoc rules — the plugin ecosystem is now the only enforcement point

require-jsdoc and valid-jsdoc were removed from ESLint core as of v9.0.0. Teams that relied on either rule for PR-time JSDoc enforcement lost it silently on upgrade, and the community's own docs now point exclusively at eslint-plugin-jsdoc to fill the gap — a plugin that's lint-only by design (it can flag a missing block, not write one).

Sources: require-jsdoc — ESLint docs · eslint-plugin-jsdoc — GitHub

The cost of catching a gap multiplies 10–100x the later it's caught

IBM Systems Sciences Institute figures, still the most-cited benchmark in 2026 shift-left coverage: a defect caught at design time costs roughly 1x to fix; at coding time, ~6.5x; at testing, ~15x; in production, ~100x. Separately, teams that shift checks left report 60–90% fewer production defects and 40–60% lower total cost of quality. The same curve applies to missing or stale documentation — caught at the PR stage it's a one-line diff; caught by a new hire three weeks into onboarding, or by a due-diligence reviewer during a raise, it's a much more expensive conversation.

Sources: Shift-Left Code Review — Qodo · Shift-Left Testing: Catching $100 Bugs Before They Become $10,000 Fires — Globalbit

Today's strongest angle combines these two: ESLint's core rule removal creates a real, timely reason for teams to look at their JSDoc enforcement setup right now, and the shift-left cost curve gives a concrete "why PR time, not release time" argument.
