3. Signal worth writing about today

"Shadow AI" is now the top code-privacy exposure category, and most teams haven't checked

A widely-cited 2025 Snyk analysis of 500 organizations found 47% had not reviewed the privacy implications of their primary AI coding tool before adopting it, and 23% later discovered the tool's data handling violated their own internal policy or a customer commitment. Separately, "shadow AI" — developers using personal AI accounts against proprietary code without security team visibility — is being described as 2026's version of shadow IT.

Sources: Privacy Considerations When Using AI Coding Tools — Vibe Coder Blog · AI Coding Tools and Public Repository Data Leaks — CybelAngel

Adoption is near-universal, but trust in the output is not

95% of surveyed developers report using AI tools at least weekly and 56% say 70%+ of their engineering work now involves AI — yet distrust of AI code-assistant output is reported as the single greatest challenge to working with AI in the dev workflow by two-thirds of developers. Widespread adoption has clearly not produced widespread confidence.

Sources: AI Tooling for Software Engineers in 2026 — Pragmatic Engineer · Challenges of AI in the development workflow — Statista

Today's strongest angle: jsdoc-scribe's whole pitch is "no AI, nothing leaves your machine" — but that has real costs too (no natural-language summarization of gnarly business logic, more manual review of edge-case wording), and an honest trade-off piece lands better with a skeptical technical audience than a one-sided pitch. This is unused backlog angle #7 ("what 'no AI' actually costs you vs. saves you"), and today's privacy/trust data gives it a timely hook. Structure (rebuttal/trade-off, arguing against your own product's easy narrative) and combined audience (founders, CTOs, and skeptical engineers together) are both unused by the five prior posts.
